Barcodes and QR codes

gLabels Plus draws barcodes as sharp vector graphics, so they scan reliably at any size.

Adding a barcode

  1. Press [[B]] (or click Barcode on the toolbar) and drag a rectangle on the label.
  2. Type the data to encode and press [[Enter]]. [[Esc]] cancels.
  3. In the Barcode tab of the properties panel, choose the barcode type.

To change the data later, double-click the barcode or press [[F2]].

Choosing a barcode type

UseRecommended type
Stock codes, SKUs, serial numbers, anything with letters and digitsCode 128 (built in, compact, any keyboard character)
Older scanners and industrial systemsCode 39 (upper-case letters, digits and - . $ / + % space)
Retail products (supermarkets, shops)EAN-13 (most of the world) or UPC-A (North America). Use the number assigned by GS1.
Cartons of retail productsITF-14 or EAN-14
Web addresses, phone numbers, Wi-Fi details, long textQR Code
Very small parts and electronicsData Matrix
Postal mailPOSTNET, USPS Intelligent Mail, or your country's postal code

Which types are available depends on the barcode libraries your copy was built with. Code 128, Code 39, UPC-A, EAN-13, POSTNET, USPS Intelligent Mail and Data Matrix are always built in. Builds with Zint, GNU Barcode and libqrencode add dozens more, including QR Code, PDF417, Aztec, Codabar, Interleaved 2 of 5 and ISBN. The barcode type menu is grouped by library.

Barcode options

  • Show text: print the human-readable text under the bars.
  • Checksum: add a check digit, for types where it is optional (for example Code 39). Types such as EAN and UPC always include one.
  • Color: the color of the bars. Black on a white label scans best.

Different barcode on every label

Click Insert substitution field in the Barcode tab and pick a merge column, for example ${SKU}. Every row of your CSV file then prints with its own barcode. See Mail merge. For sequential serial numbers without a file, use a counter variable.

Getting barcodes to scan

  • Leave a quiet zone (empty margin) on both sides of 1D barcodes, at least 10 times the width of the narrowest bar.
  • Don't squash a barcode too small. If bars merge together on a thermal printer, make the barcode wider.
  • Thermal printers print best with barcodes running in the direction the label feeds through the printer.
  • Check that the data is valid for the type: EAN-13 needs 12 or 13 digits, UPC-A needs 11 or 12.
  • Test with a phone scanning app before printing a whole batch.
If the data is not valid for the chosen type, the barcode is drawn as an empty frame or an error message instead of bars.
